Decoding Customer Value: A Data-Driven Retention Strategy
Overview

This project was completed as part of Summer Projects '26 organized by the Consulting & Analytics Club, IIT Guwahati.

The objective was to analyze customer behavior for a D2C fashion brand and identify actionable strategies to improve customer retention, reduce promotional dependency, and maximize long-term customer value.

Using a dataset containing approximately 3,900 customer records, the project combines customer analytics, SQL-driven segmentation, and business intelligence dashboarding to generate data-backed business recommendations.

Problem Statement

Businesses often struggle to distinguish:

Genuinely loyal customers
Discount-driven customers
High-value customer segments
Growth-ready geographic markets

The goal of this project was to answer:

Who are the most valuable customers?
Which customers buy only because of promotions?
What behaviors predict long-term customer value?
Which regions show strong organic demand?
What should the ideal customer profile look like?
How should promotional spending be optimized?

4. SQL Analysis

Five major business questions were explored:

Loyal vs Discount Customers

Identified genuinely loyal customers versus customers dependent on promotions.

Behavioral Drivers of Customer Value

Analyzed purchase frequency, spending patterns, and historical purchases across value tiers.

Geographic Opportunity Analysis

Identified locations with strong spending behavior and low promotional dependence.

Promotional Strategy Optimization

Evaluated where promotional spending can be reduced without impacting customer value.

Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

Built a data-backed profile of the brand's most valuable customer segment.

Key Insights
Only a small percentage of customers qualified as genuinely loyal under multiple loyalty definitions.
Purchase frequency emerged as one of the strongest predictors of customer value.
Premium customers were found who generated high revenue while remaining heavily dependent on discounts.
Several geographic regions demonstrated high spending despite low promotional dependency, indicating strong organic demand.
Customer retention strategies should focus on increasing purchase frequency and reducing discount dependency among high-value segments.
